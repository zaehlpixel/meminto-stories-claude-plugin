# Adapt the start to the book

Choose only the relevant row. These examples are optional ways into the user's own material, not facts to insert and not a list to ask all at once. The actual book, user goal and chosen writing style take priority. Do not infer ages, dates, beliefs, relationship status, loss or a fixed definition of family from a template name.

| Topic | A useful opening direction | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Life book | A place or small turning point the author still remembers clearly. | Let them start anywhere; chronology can come later. |
| Life book for Christians | An experience, person or practice that mattered to their faith. | Use the author's own beliefs and language; do not add doctrine or interpret an event as divine intervention. |
| Childhood / youth | An ordinary scene, a first experience or a phrase worth keeping. | Establish who is writing and whose perspective it is. A parent can describe what they saw without claiming to know the child's thoughts. |
| Relationship | A shared routine, first encounter or moment that captures the relationship. | Preserve the speaker's perspective. Attribute a second partner's supplied words instead of inventing agreement or a shared "we" voice. |
| Wedding | A scene from preparing, celebrating or looking back on the day. | Ask only for relevant context; do not assume the wedding has already happened or that the writer is one of the couple. |
| Memorial / condolence | A characteristic gesture, saying or everyday memory of the person. | Follow the user's emotional pace; offer lighter memories or a pause. Do not push for details of death or claim a therapeutic outcome. |
| Family yearbook | A small moment from a chosen month, season or family event. | A quiet month is allowed. Do not invent an event or demand equal coverage of every month or family member. |
| Travel | A particular arrival, unexpected encounter, taste or place. | Preserve the route and dates actually known. Do not turn generic destination knowledge into personal experience. |
| Dog memory book | A habit, first meeting or small adventure with the dog. | Ask about the animal's actual story; do not assume bereavement or invent the dog's inner thoughts. |
| Blank template / any custom topic | A person, place, collection, interest, project or experience the author wants to preserve. | Start from purpose and material, not predefined questions. Recipes, club history, hobbies or fiction are examples of possible custom projects, not claims about preset templates. |

For a completely undecided blank-book author, a German opening could be:

"Wir können ganz klein anfangen: mit einem Menschen oder Tier, einem Moment oder etwas, das du weitergeben möchtest. Was davon spricht dich am ehesten an? Ein ganz anderes Thema geht natürlich auch."

If they want no questions, replace that with an invitation:

"Du brauchst noch keinen Plan. Nimm ein Foto oder einen Gegenstand und notiere einfach ein paar Wörter dazu. Daraus können wir deinen ersten kleinen Abschnitt formen."

Use these in the user's language and adapt them to what they have already told you. Offer two to four tentative headings only after a direction emerges. Do not always start with "My childhood", require a full life arc, or promise automatic access to templates, the question library or new-book creation.
